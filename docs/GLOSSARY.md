# Cognitia Glossary

A standardized reference of domain-neutral cognitive and architectural terminology.

---

### Observation
A raw or structured data record captured from a sensor, telemetry stream, or environment node at a specific instant in time.

### Evidence
An observation or collection of observations evaluated specifically to support, challenge, or refute an epistemic proposition (Claim, Hypothesis, Model) carrying directional attribution and confidence.

### Experience
An episodic record encapsulating context, observation, action taken, expected outcome, actual outcome, and provenance.

### Hypothesis
A formal, testable proposition regarding latent system state, causal relationships, or environment dynamics.

### Claim
An asserted cognitive statement with an associated epistemic confidence score and status.

### Confidence
An advisory normalized score in the interval $[0.0, 1.0]$. Its interpretation is subsystem-specific and MUST NOT automatically be treated as a calibrated probability, truth value, or measurement certainty:
* **Claim Confidence**: Degree of epistemic support based on available evidence.
* **Evidence Confidence**: Source reliability / corroboration strength of the underlying observation(s).
* **Decision Confidence**: Provider/capability certainty in the proposed advisory action.
* **Reasoning Confidence**: Logical validity or step reliability of an inferential derivation.

### Epistemic State
The current evaluation and belief classification of a cognitive proposition (`UNKNOWN`, `OBSERVED`, `HYPOTHESIS`, `TESTABLE`, `SUPPORTED`, `REFUTED`, `UNRESOLVED`).

### Epistemic Transition
An append-only versioned record representing a change in status or confidence of an epistemic node along with rationale and provenance.

### Transition Outcome
The review, governance, or approval outcome (`REVISION_PROPOSED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`) associated with model lifecycle evolution (Phase 2 governance).

### Reasoning
Structured, deliberate cognitive derivation (deductive, abductive, analogical, counterfactual, or causal) producing an immutable audit trace (`ReasoningTrace`).

### ReasoningInput
An immutable, self-contained cognitive snapshot capturing context identity, attention results, selected items, premises, active rules, constraints, interventions, and causal graphs required for deterministic reasoning without live-store lookups.

### ReasoningResult
An immutable container capturing candidate conclusions, candidate hypotheses, candidate claims, residuals, and detected contradictions produced by a reasoning derivation.

### ReasoningResidual
An explicit record of an unknown, missing observation, missing transition rule, or unmodeled dynamic encountered during reasoning derivation.

### Deductive Conclusion
The deterministic outcome of rule-based deduction capturing matched rule IDs, satisfied conditions, unsatisfied conditions, derived attributes, and residuals.

### Abductive Hypotheses
A collection of candidate explanatory hypotheses generated for observed symptoms, deterministically ranked by symptom coverage without fabricated probabilities.

### Analogical Mapping
A structural alignment between a source pattern and target context capturing correspondences, similarity basis, and explicit limitations without asserting semantic or physical equivalence.

### Causal Hypothesis
An explicit proposition relating cause and effect via a mechanism evaluated against observed evidence. Causality is never inferred from temporal order or correlation alone.

### Counterfactual Scenario
A constrained hypothetical scenario derived from baseline state and explicit interventions using supplied deterministic transition rules; explicitly marked as non-observed simulation.

### Reasoning Strategy
A modular SPI protocol (`ReasoningStrategy`) implementing a specific reasoning mode (`DeductiveReasoner`, `AbductiveReasoner`, `AnalogicalReasoner`, `CausalReasoner`, `CounterfactualReasoner`).

### Reasoning Engine
A provider-neutral orchestration layer (`ReasoningEngine`, `DeterministicReasoningEngine`) that validates snapshots, selects strategies, captures traces, and links provenance.

### Decision
A cognitive recommendation or proposal generated by a capability in response to an observation or context. Purely advisory; carries direct provenance attachment.

### Capability
A provider-agnostic cognitive function specification (e.g., decision-making, reasoning, prediction) implementing standard SPI protocols.

### Provider
A concrete computational engine or component (e.g., deterministic rules, heuristics, ML model, neural network, human, or Laya) implementing one or more Cognitia capability contracts.

### Provider Boundary
The architectural boundary separating generic Cognitia contracts and SPIs from concrete cognitive engines, models, and external providers.

### Model
A versioned computational artifact (weights, parameters, lookup tables, rule matrices) registered through the Model Registry and associated with a specific provider.

### Provenance
The immutable audit trail documenting creator, source type, inputs, parent references, schema versions, timestamps, and checksums.

### Persistence Plane
The durable architectural substrate responsible for recording cognitive events and preserving canonical cognitive artifacts without becoming an authority.

### Cognitive Memory
A structured retrieval layer over persisted cognitive history that selects historically relevant observations, experiences, evidence, reasoning traces, decisions, outcomes, and epistemic states for subsequent cognitive processing.

### Cognitive Context
A structured situational assembly of historical memories, temporal neighbors, active rules, and epistemic states relevant to a reference observation.

### Cognitive Attention
The deterministic subsystem responsible for selecting, prioritizing, and focusing information from an assembled `CognitiveContext` for a specific cognitive task. Context assembles; Attention prioritizes.

### Advanced Capability Type
A canonical classification of advanced cognitive capability (`LLM_REASONING`, `TINYML_REASONING`, `HYPOTHESIS_GENERATION`, `CAUSAL_INFERENCE`, `SEMANTIC_GRAPH`, `VECTOR_RETRIEVAL`, `REINFORCEMENT_LEARNING`, `SELF_MODIFYING_REASONING`).

### Provider Lifecycle Status
The formal lifecycle state of an advanced provider (`DECLARED`, `REGISTERED`, `VALIDATED`, `AVAILABLE`, `ENABLED`, `DISABLED`, `SUSPENDED`, `RETIRED`).

### Proposal Lifecycle Status
The governance lifecycle for candidate structures produced by providers (`PROPOSED`, `VALIDATED`, `APPROVED`, `ACTIVATABLE`, `ACTIVATED`, `REJECTED`, `RETIRED`).

### Advanced Provider Record
An immutable record capturing the identity, version, capabilities, resource requirements, authority level, lifecycle status, and provenance of an advanced provider.

### Candidate Reasoning Artifact
The universal envelope for outputs emitted by advanced providers. New artifacts start with `epistemic_status=UNRESOLVED` and `proposal_status=PROPOSED`. They are advisory proposals, never truth or authority.

### Provider Gateway
The execution authorization layer that verifies provider existence, lifecycle status (`ENABLED`), capability support, resource satisfaction, and snapshot validity before allowing provider execution. Does NOT perform epistemic evaluation.

### Reasoning Request
An immutable request envelope for invoking an advanced reasoning provider, referencing `input_snapshot_id`, `reasoning_mode`, `requested_capability`, and `configuration`.

### Resource Requirements
An immutable declaration of computational and environment dependencies (`requires_network`, `requires_gpu`, `requires_external_runtime`, `requires_filesystem`, `memory_bytes_limit`, `sandbox_type`).

### Advanced Intelligence
The optional provider layer that augments deterministic Cognitia cognition with scaffolded advanced capabilities. Core Cognitia remains fully deterministic and functional without any advanced provider present or enabled.

### AVAILABLE vs ENABLED
An advanced provider may be `REGISTERED`, `VALIDATED`, `AVAILABLE`, and still `DISABLED`. `AVAILABLE` indicates resources are present; `ENABLED` is the explicit permission to execute. These states are distinct and both must be satisfied for execution.

### Candidate vs Truth
Provider outputs (`CandidateReasoningArtifact`) are never truth, decisions, or authority. They require explicit epistemic evaluation, governance review, and explicit activation before influencing production cognition.

### Provider Execution vs Epistemic Evaluation
Provider execution produces candidate artifacts. Epistemic evaluation is a separate, explicit downstream step. They are never automatically coupled.
